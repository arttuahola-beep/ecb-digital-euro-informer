Monday 9 November 2026 Banks

EU lawmakers aim to settle the digital euro law on 18 November, so prepare your plan for the three issues still open

The digital euro law is not adopted yet. It is still a Commission proposal (COM(2023) 369). The Council set its position in December 2025 and the European Parliament in July 2026. Talks between them are now under way, and the Irish Council Presidency hopes for a political deal on Wednesday 18 November. The ECB says that if the law is done by the end of 2026, it aims to be ready for a first issuance in 2029.

Three open points matter most for banks. First, how merchant fees will be set, including Parliament's "no worse off" clause, which France opposes. Second, whether people under sanctions by non-EU countries can get digital euro services. Third, the technical rules on which payment providers must offer the digital euro. Each one can change your costs, your onboarding checks, or whether you must offer the service at all.

Do not wait for the final text to start thinking. Before 18 November, write a short note for your board with two or three outcomes for each open point and what each would mean for your fees, compliance work and project budget. When the deal is announced, check the text against that note and update your plan within days, not months. A political deal is not the final law: the legal text still needs formal adoption by both Parliament and Council.

Source: ECB, keynote speech by Piero Cipollone, \"Shaping the future of money\", Tirana, 5 November 2026; Agence Europe, 6 November 2026 (trilogue report); Commission proposal COM(2023) 369
