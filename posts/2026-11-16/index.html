Monday 16 November 2026 Banks

Banks that must offer the digital euro will also have to help older and less digital customers, so design that support now

Under the Commission's proposal, credit institutions that run payment accounts must offer basic digital euro services to euro-area residents who ask for them. The same proposal says every provider with that duty should give digital inclusion support to people with disabilities, functional limitations or limited digital skills, and to older people. The digital euro itself is meant to be designed in line with the European Accessibility Act. This is still a proposal: EU lawmakers are negotiating the final text and aim for a political deal this week, so details may change.

The proposal also asks each euro-area member state to name public bodies, such as local or regional authorities or post offices, to give basic digital euro services to people who do not want an account at a bank or other provider. That does not take your duty away. Your own customers who are elderly or less confident online will still come to your branches and call centres first.

So plan the support model now, not after launch. Check that your digital euro app screens, card and offline options meet accessibility rules. Train branch and phone staff to set up and explain a digital euro account step by step. Decide how you will help people who cannot use a smartphone. Budget for it, because basic services for individuals must be free. Clear, simple support can also win you trust with customers your competitors overlook.

Source: European Commission proposal for a Regulation on the establishment of the digital euro, COM(2023) 369 (explanatory memorandum and recital on distribution by public entities; Art. 14 distribution duty; Arts. 22–24 technical features); Directive (EU) 2019/882 (European Accessibility Act)
