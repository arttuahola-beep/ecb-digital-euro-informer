Digital euro merchant fees may differ by country for years, so model your card costs market by market

Negotiators have still not agreed how digital euro merchant fees will be capped. Council minutes from the 18–19 November trilogue, reported by Börsen-Zeitung on 25 November, show two open questions. Should fee caps be the same across the euro area, or vary by country? And when should the temporary caps give way to a permanent model? More talks are planned for 3 and 16 December. None of this is law yet.

The Council's working approach shows where this could go. For the first five years, caps on merchant fees and inter-PSP fees would follow a euro-area average for comparable payments, meaning national and international debit cards. Where a country's own average is clearly lower, the lower national cap would apply. After five years, the Commission would check every year whether a "cost-plus" model can take over, and the whole fee model would be reviewed after ten years. Parliament wants stronger protection so merchants are no worse off.

What to do now: if you sell in several euro countries, don't plan on one digital euro rate. Model your expected fees for each country using today's local debit card costs. Ask your acquirers to quote digital euro pricing country by country. In new contracts, add a review clause tied to the final law and to the later move to a permanent fee model.
