Tuesday 10 November 2026 Payment service providers

The digital euro is meant to be a shared acceptance layer for the whole euro area, so plan where your brand could grow on it

In a speech on 5 November, ECB Board member Piero Cipollone said the digital euro would create "a common public infrastructure and acceptance layer for European payments". Payment firms could build on it and use its open standards to offer services across the euro area. He also said the Eurosystem would not charge scheme or processing fees. And he pointed to a gap: 13 euro area countries rely entirely on non-European companies for in-store card payments.

For a payment firm, this is a growth question, not only a compliance task. If your wallet, app or local scheme works in one country today, ask where a shared euro-area acceptance network could take it. Which countries and merchant types would you target first? Which features would you build on top, since basic use would be free for consumers and your margin must come from extra services? Write down two or three markets and the services you would lead with.

Keep the status clear. The digital euro law is still a proposal under negotiation, and the ECB aims for a first issuance in 2029 only if the law is agreed by the end of 2026. The pilot is planned for the second half of 2027. So treat this as strategy work now: a short plan, a rough budget and a go/no-go point once the law is final.

Source: ECB, Piero Cipollone, \"Shaping the future of money\", keynote speech, Tirana, 5 November 2026; proposed Regulation on the digital euro, COM(2023) 369 (in trilogue)
