If your company bills customers again and again, the digital euro is being built with you in mind. The ECB's draft rulebook (v0.91, July 2026) has sections for standing orders and recurring payments, pre-authorisations, and refunds. A standing order is a fixed, regular payment that the payer's provider sends automatically. A recurring payment is one where the merchant stores the payer's details and the payer agrees in advance to terms such as amount, frequency and end date. A pre-authorisation is for cases where the final amount is not known yet, like a fuel pump. The customer can never be charged more than the amount they approved.

The practical step is to list your billing flows now: subscriptions, instalments, deposits, hotel or rental holds, and returns. Match each one to the right digital euro payment type. Then ask your bank or acquirer three things. Will they support all of these types from day one? How will customers change or cancel a mandate? How will refunds reach your linked account and your books?

Keep in mind what is still open. The rulebook is a draft. The ECB says it is not for pilot or implementation use. The digital euro law is still in trilogue, with a political round set for 18 November 2026. Plan your flows now, but do not lock in contracts until the final rules are out.
