Thursday 26 November 2026 Payment service providers

Pilot rules say users must be able to block a lost phone or card themselves, so build that self-service flow now

The ECB's pilot rules for payment firms set out what must happen when a customer loses a phone or an offline digital euro device. Pilot PSPs must let people block, unblock, add and remove their own payment instruments, such as an app or an offline device. Before blocking, the PSP must check that the person reporting the loss really is the authorised user.

Once blocked, the instrument cannot pay, receive, fund, defund or even check transactions. If the customer finds it again, the PSP must switch it back on. For offline devices there is an extra step: when the device next goes online, the PSP must check whether it was reported lost or stolen and, if so, disable it.

These are pilot requirements, not final law, but they show where the scheme is heading. Plan the build now: a block button in your app and online banking, a fast identity check for loss reports by phone or in a branch, and a back-end flag that reaches offline devices when they reconnect. Test the "found it again" path too, so honest customers are not locked out for days.

Source: ECB, Digital euro pilot – PSP Participation Agreement, Appendix 2.B Functional Requirements, v2.0 (14 April 2026, republished 12 May 2026), rules AM-021-017 to AM-021-021 (pilot requirements, not the final scheme rulebook or adopted law)
