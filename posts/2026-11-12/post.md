The ECB says it will make all main digital euro uses technically available from day one: paying another person, paying online and paying in a shop. But market feedback shows a gradual rollout would make the start smoother. On 10 November the ECB said it welcomes that EU lawmakers intend to give legal clarity on a phased rollout in shops. This is not law yet. The digital euro rules are still being negotiated, and the next trilogue meeting is set for 18 November 2026. First issuance is targeted for 2029 only if the law is adopted by the end of 2026.

For a company that sells to consumers, this means your shop tills, web checkout and mobile app may not all need digital euro on the same date. Do not plan for one big switch-on, and do not assume you will get a long grace period either. List each sales channel, count its terminals and payment flows, and ask your acquirer and checkout providers which phase each channel would likely fall into once the rules are final. Then spread your budget and testing across those dates instead of one deadline.

If you run an online or app-based business and want early hands-on experience, note that the ECB opened a call in September for online and mobile merchants to test remote payments in the 2027 pilot. The final merchant selection is due early next year. The pilot uses a beta version with no legal tender status, so treat it as a learning step, not a go-live.
