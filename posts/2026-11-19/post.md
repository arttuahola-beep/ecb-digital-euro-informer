The digital euro law is still a proposal. One point the negotiators have not settled is sanctions. After the 5 November trilogue, it was still unclear whether people sanctioned by a non-EU country could get digital euro services. The co-legislators are waiting for guidance from the European Commission. Talks went on on 18 November, but no agreed text has been published yet.

For banks, the practical point is clear. Under the proposal, you would open and run digital euro accounts, so the screening duty would sit with you. EU sanctions and anti-money laundering rules apply to you either way. What is still open is how far third-country lists should count. Do not wait for the final wording. Make sure your onboarding and payment screening can run digital euro wallets through the same sanctions engine as your current accounts. Also make it easy to switch extra lists on or off by rule.

A good test is to take one sanctioned-customer case and run it from start to finish: a blocked onboarding, a frozen wallet, a rejected payment and a report to your authority. Record where the digital euro flow differs from your card or account flow. When the final text arrives, you will only need to change settings, not rebuild the system.
