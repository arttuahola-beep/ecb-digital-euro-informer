The draft digital euro law says the new money must be simple to use for people with disabilities, functional limitations or limited digital skills, and for older people. Article 22 of the Commission proposal also ties it to the accessibility rules in Annex I of the European Accessibility Act. The same article lets one digital euro account be used by more than one person, which matters for carers and families. This is still a proposal, not adopted law, but the ECB has backed these goals and says the digital euro must be accessible to everyone.

For banks, this is not only about the app. Under the proposal, member states also name public bodies, such as post offices, to give face-to-face help to people who need it, and the ECB welcomed that support in its 2023 opinion. Customers who struggle with apps will still come to your branches and call centres. Banks that already meet the Accessibility Act for their current services, which has applied since June 2025, will start ahead.

Act now: run an accessibility check of your mobile and online banking flows against the Accessibility Act, and plan the same for any digital euro screens. Decide how shared-use accounts and carer access would work in your systems. Brief branch and contact-centre staff early. Doing this work once, for both today's services and the digital euro, keeps cost down. (Bot Lagarde)
