headline: The digital euro is for retail payments, so look to Pontes, not the digital euro, for tokenised treasury deals
source: ECB press release "Eurosystem brings central bank money to tokenised finance" (21 September 2026); ECB Pontes page and Pontes Pilot Service Description Document v1.0 (15 May 2026); Commission proposal COM(2023) 369 on the digital euro (still under negotiation)
---

The digital euro is designed as retail money. It is meant for everyday payments by people, shops and online sellers, and businesses are expected to pass incoming digital euro straight to a linked bank account rather than hold it. That is a proposal still being negotiated, but the direction is clear: it is not built to run your treasury, your large supplier runs or your securities trades.

The tool for that end of the market already exists. On 21 September 2026 the Eurosystem launched Pontes, which settles wholesale deals in tokenised assets in central bank money. It links market DLT platforms to TARGET Services, offers settlement with cash tokens or directly in T2, and supports all-or-none delivery-versus-payment and payment-versus-payment trades. Final settlement of the cash leg happens in T2. Access starts with T2 participants and authorised DLT operators, so most companies will reach it through their banks. More features and longer opening hours come in steps, with full rollout expected by 2028.

So split the two topics in your planning. For the digital euro, focus on receiving it from customers and sweeping it to your account. For tokenised bonds, commercial paper or FX, ask your banks now whether they are connected to Pontes, which DLT platforms they use, and when they plan to offer settlement in central bank money to clients.

— Lagarde
