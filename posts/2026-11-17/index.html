The ECB has already modelled digital euro deposit outflows, so run that stress case through your own liquidity numbers now

Lawmakers asked the ECB how much money could leave bank deposits under holding limits of up to €3,000 per person. The ECB's answer gives banks a ready-made stress case. In normal use, the impact on deposits is small. In an extreme "flight-to-safety" case, where everyone fills their wallet to the limit, outflows rise from €156 billion at a €500 limit to €699 billion at €3,000. That is 2.2% of banking assets, or 8.2% of retail sight deposits. The ECB found that small market lenders, retail lenders and diversified lenders would feel it most.

The holding limit is not set yet. Under the draft law, the ECB would fix it later, and the regulation is still being negotiated. But you do not need the final number to prepare. Take the ECB's two scenarios and apply them to your own retail deposit base at €500, €1,500 and €3,000. Check what each one does to your LCR, your NSFR and your funding plan.

Then put the results to work. Share them with your ALM and treasury teams. Use them in your internal liquidity stress testing (ILAAP). Bring them to supervisory talks, so you are not starting from scratch once the limit is announced.

Source: ECB, "Technical data on the financial stability impact of the digital euro" (technical annex for co-legislators, October 2025); Commission proposal COM(2023) 369, Art. 16 (holding limits, still a proposal)
