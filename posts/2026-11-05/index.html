Thursday 5 November 2026 Payment service providers

Pilot banks stay fully responsible for their tech vendors, so tighten your outsourcing contracts before you build

Many pilot payment service providers will lean on technical service providers, such as processors, software vendors and integrators. The ECB allows this. But those vendors have no contract with the ECB or the Eurosystem. The PSP signs the participation agreement with its national central bank and stays fully responsible and liable, whatever it outsources. The ECB points to the EBA outsourcing guidelines as the standard.

The vendor can still do real work. The ECB says an authorised vendor may send and receive pseudonymised data from the digital euro service platform on your behalf, such as payment initiation, status updates and balance queries. The PSP remains answerable for every interaction. If something fails across several parties, the ECB will judge it case by case, based on the contracts each party holds. Your contract with the vendor is the only one that covers that gap.

So review vendor terms now. Match their service levels, incident duties, audit rights and data handling to what you owe your central bank. This is the pilot FAQ, not law, and it does not bind later phases. Still, the same set-up is the best guide to what an outsourcing-heavy digital euro rollout will need.

Source: ECB, FAQs on the digital euro pilot (updated 28 October 2026), Q46 and Q53–55 and Q59; EBA Guidelines on outsourcing arrangements
