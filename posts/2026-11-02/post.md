headline: "Basic digital euro services must be free for people, so decide now what you will charge for and what you will not"
source: "Commission proposal for a Regulation on the establishment of the digital euro, COM(2023) 369 final, Articles 14 and 17 and Annex II (proposal, still in trilogue)"
---

Under the Commission's draft law, banks that run payment accounts must offer basic digital euro services to their customers on request. People must get these basic services free of charge. Annex II lists them: opening, holding and closing a digital euro account, checking balances and transactions, manual funding and defunding from a normal account or from cash, at least one payment instrument, and paying or getting paid person to person, in shops and online, and to or from the government. Standing orders are in the free set. Other conditional payments are not.

This is still a proposal. Parliament and Council are negotiating it, and some MEPs have proposed amendments, for example that no new services should be added to the free list later. But the core rule is not new: the free basic list has been part of the draft from the start. Fees you may charge for other services also sit next to the separate caps on merchant fees.

The takeaway for banks: map each digital euro feature you plan to offer to either the free basic list or a paid extra. Price the free part as a cost of serving retail customers, and build your business case on paid add-ons such as automated funding rules, pay-on-delivery or business tools. Doing this early helps you avoid launching a feature you later find you cannot charge for.
