The latest round of EU talks on the digital euro ended on 19 November with no deal. Negotiators spent a long time on how payment service providers will be paid for offering it, and on possible exceptions to the duty for shops to accept it. More talks are planned for 3 and 16 December, and the Council wants a deal before the end of the year. So the payment model is still not settled.

What is on the table today is the Commission's proposal. It would cap the fees that merchants pay and the fees that payment firms charge each other. The ECB would set those caps based on costs, a reasonable profit and the price of similar payment methods. Until a final text is published, none of these numbers or rules are fixed.

For banks, the lesson is to plan for more than one outcome. Build a simple revenue model for your digital euro service with a few fee scenarios, from tight caps to looser ones. Compare each one with what you earn from debit cards today. Then you can sign off your budget quickly once the law is agreed, without having to start over.
