headline: "Digital euro access outside the euro area needs official deals first, so build residency checks into your onboarding now"
source: "Proposal for a Regulation on the establishment of the digital euro, COM(2023) 369, Arts. 18–20 and recitals 47–49; companion proposal COM(2023) 368, Art. 3 (both still under negotiation)"
---

Under the draft digital euro law, who you may serve depends on where they live. Banks in the European Economic Area could serve euro-area residents and visitors to the euro area directly. But people and firms living in EU countries that do not use the euro could only get digital euro after their central bank signs an arrangement with the ECB, at that country's request. For residents of countries outside the EU, the EU must first sign an agreement with that country, plus an ECB arrangement with its central bank. Agreements with high-risk third countries can be restricted, suspended or ended.

This matters for your onboarding and account rules. You will need to know a customer's residence at sign-up and track changes over time, for example when a customer moves abroad. A companion proposal lets banks in non-euro EU countries serve euro-area residents, visitors, and people who opened a digital euro account while living in the euro area and then moved away. Banks in the Nordics, the Baltics and Central Europe should map which of their customers would fall into each group.

Remember these are proposals, not adopted law. EU lawmakers aim for a political deal later this month, and the rules on non-euro use may still change. Practical step now: add a residency flag and a 'visitor' category to your digital euro customer data model, and plan a check that can switch access on or off per country once any arrangements are signed.
