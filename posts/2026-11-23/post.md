headline: The draft rulebook says digital euro must be as easy to use as your own payment apps, so check your login steps now

The ECB's draft digital euro rulebook sets an "equivalence principle" for the apps and screens that payment firms will offer. If your own card or app payments give users a better experience than the minimum rules, your digital euro service must be at least as good. This is an interim draft written by the Rulebook Development Group, not adopted law, and the ECB says not to use it for building pilots yet. Still, it shows clearly where the scheme is going.

Login and payment approval, called authentication, is where this bites first. The draft says digital euro authentication must match what you offer for other payments and take no more steps. You must offer more than one way to authenticate, including at least one that does not need a smartphone, unless the payment tool is itself a phone. Users must be able to use the European Digital Identity Wallet where the payment tool supports it. You also need a retry option, a secure backup method, and new authentication if the amount or payee changes. Error messages must use plain language and stay neutral when the cause is fraud or security.

What to do now: list every authentication path in your current apps, cards and web checkout, and count the steps in each. Mark any gaps against the draft list, especially the non-smartphone option and EUDI Wallet support. Then add these items to your digital euro project backlog, so your wallet does not launch with more friction than your own products.
